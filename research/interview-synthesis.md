# User Interview Synthesis — Retention Research

Source: 3 interviews — Priya S. (power user, 18 months), Tom R. (churned, left after 5 weeks), Amara L. (new user, 10 days in).

## Top 5 Themes

### 1. No actionable next step after the initial insight
Users repeatedly describe getting a strong first insight (spending breakdown) but then having no idea what to do with it.

- Amara: "I have been waiting for Nudge to tell me the next step."
- Tom: "I kept waiting for it to give me something to act on and it never really did."

### 2. The experience feels static regardless of tenure
The app doesn't visibly change or respond as users spend more time on it, which reads as the product being "done" showing them anything new.

- Tom: "The app just showed me the same dashboard every time."
- Amara: "I check it occasionally but nothing has changed since the first day."

### 3. Personalized pattern recognition is what creates long-term stickiness
For the retained power user, the moment Nudge started surfacing non-obvious personal patterns is what turned it into a habit.

- Priya: "It started surfacing patterns I had not consciously registered — like that I always overspend in the last week of the month."
- Priya: "I feel like Nudge actually knows my financial habits now."

### 4. Time-to-value is longer than most users' patience
The payoff that hooked the power user took months to arrive — far longer than the window in which the churned and new users were already losing patience.

- Priya: "It took about three months to get there and I think most people give up before that."
- Tom (churned at 5 weeks): "I really wanted it to work... but after the initial setup it felt like nothing changed."

### 5. Users want the app to actively demand something of them, not just display data
Passive dashboards read as inert. Users associate value with being asked to do something, not just shown something.

- Tom: "I switched to YNAB because at least that feels like it is asking something of me."
- Amara: "The spending breakdown was shocking in a useful way... but now I do not really know what to do with that information."

## Contradictions & Tensions

- **Passive discovery vs. active demand:** Priya's stickiness came from Nudge quietly *surfacing* patterns for her — a passive experience. Tom explicitly wanted the opposite: an app that *asks something of him*. What retained the power user is close to the opposite of what the churned user says he needed. This suggests the right mode may differ by tenure — early users may need to be actively prompted/asked, while long-tenured users are content with quiet, passive insight delivery once trust is established.
- **The "aha moment" is necessary but insufficient:** Amara had the same kind of strong initial insight (subscription spending shock) that likely hooked Priya early on, but for Amara it dead-ended. The insight alone didn't sustain engagement — only Priya's continued, deepening pattern discovery did.
- **Value timeline vs. churn timeline don't match:** Priya says real payoff takes ~3 months and "most people give up before that." Tom churned at 5 weeks, Amara is already stalling at 10 days. The product's real value-delivery timeline is structurally incompatible with how long most users actually stick around.

## Single Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

The deep personalization that creates long-term stickiness (Priya's pattern recognition) genuinely takes months to develop — but users are deciding whether Nudge is "working" within days to weeks. Week-1 retention isn't a smaller version of the 18-month retention problem; it requires a different mechanism entirely. Nudge needs an early, manufactured sense of momentum — a concrete, actionable next step delivered within the first week (not a fully mature personalized insight engine) — that gives users something to *do*, not just something to *see*. Without that bridge, users interpret the static dashboard as evidence the app has nothing more to offer, and churn well before the product's real value has a chance to emerge.
